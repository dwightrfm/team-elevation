Max Out Referrals

You will not survive in business without a 100% commitment to getting referrals.

THE ASK — ask three different ways, every time.
  1. Who do you know that could benefit from this type of education or use help financially?
  2. Our company is in a huge expansion mode. Who do you know that is good with people, maybe wants to make extra money, or is looking for a career change?
  3. What I'd like to do also is build referral outlets for [trainee]. Who do you know that we can give referrals to? Anyone you refer would love you for it — an accountant or bookkeeper, a mortgage broker, someone in sales. We can send referrals to grow their business in hopes they can send referrals back.

If they're having trouble, go over the Memory Jogger and help them think of names.

Where referrals come from

  • From new business partners
  • Client referrals from your warm market
  • Center of influence (COI) referrals

Running it in the field

  • Goal: a minimum of 8–10 client referrals.
  • Trainee and trainer work together to get referrals.
  • Pre-frame the prospect that you'll be asking — don't spring it at the end.
  • Let them know you don't do business on the first appointment. They can relax and learn.
  • Tell them the business is built 100% by referrals, and to be thinking of people who might benefit.
  • Let them know you're looking for quality people for the business.
  • Get a commitment from them to give you referrals.
  • Use the client memory jogger and referral sheets.
  • Teach the prospect to 3-way text the person referred, and to edify you.
  • Call right away while it's still a hot referral. Friendly tone.

Where it lands in the appointment

Beginning of the 1st appointment. Tonight we'll cover who we are and what we do, but look at it three ways: these concepts might benefit your family, so ask questions; our company is expanding and looking for good people; and maybe you can think of people who could use the help.

During the presentation. "Don't you think more people need to know more about this?" "How many people do you know who are in this type of situation?"

End of the presentation. If I can show you a way to get higher returns, minimize taxes, and guarantee you won't lose money — how many people do you think would want to know that information?

After info-gather. We don't do traditional marketing. We look for introductions to good people from good people like you. So if you could think of 5–10 people that [trainee] and I could get in front of, that would be great. Does that sound fair?

If hesitant. Have you felt comfortable with the way I've handled myself with you? Great — I can guarantee you one thing: I'm going to treat the people you introduce me to with the exact same respect I've treated you. Does that sound good?

Start of the 2nd appointment. Any questions since last time? I just wanted to review what we talked about — coming to our open house and introducing us to about 10 people. We're still good for that?

After the plan and paperwork. Isn't this great? (Highlight the FIN number and long-term projections, the difference in their future.) Then do THE ASK again — all three ways.

Center of Influence (COI)

A COI is someone who is not part of your business, but likes what you do and gives you referrals. A COI is a leader or a credible person. Develop relationships over time with many COIs.

Touch 3 COIs per month, minimum. Every held appointment ends with an ask — even a no. A no to the business still owes referrals.
